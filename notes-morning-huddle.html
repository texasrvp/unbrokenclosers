The two-minute morning huddle that actually trains

By LJ Stephens · 3 min read

Most sales meetings go the same way: yesterday's numbers, today's specials, a reminder about the CRM, and a "let's have a big day." Nobody leaves better at selling than they walked in.

You don't need an hour to fix that. You need two minutes, used on purpose.

The format

  1. One skill (15 seconds). Name a single, specific moment: "Today: answering 'I need to talk to my spouse.'"
  2. One example (30 seconds). Say the response out loud, exactly as you'd want a rep to say it.
  3. One rep (60 seconds). Pick someone. You play the customer. They answer. Coach one thing, then run it again.
  4. One challenge (15 seconds). "Use it on a real customer today. Tell me at close how it went."

Why it works

Skills come from repetition, not information. Research on sales coaching keeps finding the same pattern: structured, regular coaching beats occasional pep talks. In one study of 1,200 companies, 62.3% of reps met quota where there was a formal coaching process, against 50.3% where it was left to managers (LevelEleven).

A two-minute huddle is the smallest formal coaching process there is. Run it every day for a month and each rep gets twenty focused repetitions they would never have gotten otherwise.

Make it stick

  • Rotate who gets picked, so everyone stays sharp.
  • Keep a list of the skills you've covered and repeat the important ones every few weeks.
  • When someone uses the skill on a real customer and it works, say so in front of the team the next morning.

That last point matters more than it looks. Recognition is what turns a drill into a habit.
