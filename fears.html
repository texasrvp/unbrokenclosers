The 7 Sales Fears

What happened in your past can quietly cap your income. Find the fear that’s holding you back, and take your first step past it.

Every one of us carries something from the past: the house we grew up in, the neighborhood, the family, a loss, a failure. It shapes how we think, how we talk and how we show up in front of a customer. Left alone, it turns into fear, and fear turns into habits that cap your income.

Unbroken doesn’t mean you never broke. It means you refused to let what happened in your life define you.

The Fear Finder

14 quick statements, about two minutes. Answer for how you really sell, not how you wish you did.

1

The Fear of Making Money

“Who am I to ask for that much?”

Where it can come from

Maybe you grew up hearing that money is the root of all evil, or that people with money are greedy. Maybe money was the reason for the fights in your house, or you’d be the first in your family to earn more, and part of you feels guilty about it.

What it does on the sales floor

You discount before anyone asks. You rush past the price. You feel a little guilty about your commission, and after a big month you somehow slow down.

What it costs you

You leave money on the table every week. And if you can’t make a living, you can’t stay in the business long enough to help the people who need you.

Your first step

Write down three things money would let you do for the people you love. Then present the full price first on every deal this week, out loud and without apologizing.

2

The Fear of Being Successful

“If I make it, people will change on me.”

Where it can come from

Maybe you watched someone who “made it” get resented by the people around them. Maybe you were told not to get too big for your britches. Or part of you worries that if you succeed, someone will find out you don’t really belong there.

What it does on the sales floor

You coast after a good month. You set goals you know you can hit. You avoid the top of the board, and you pull back right before a breakthrough.

What it costs you

You plateau. You stay average when you were built for more, and the people you could have led never get to see what’s possible.

Your first step

Write one goal that makes your heart race, the kind you’d write on a million-dollar bill. Tell one person you trust what it is.

3

The Fear of Cold-Calling

“I don’t want to bother them.”

Where it can come from

Maybe you were raised not to talk to strangers or impose on people. Maybe the phone ringing at your house meant bad news or bill collectors. Or someone hung up on you early, and it stuck.

What it does on the sales floor

You find busywork before you dial. You wait for walk-ins and leads to come to you. When you do call, you rush the first line and hope they say no fast.

What it costs you

Your income depends on luck and on who walks in. Slow weeks hit harder, and you can’t control your own month.

Your first step

Write your first sentence down. Make ten calls before 10 a.m., and tell yourself before each one: “I’m not bothering them. I’m offering help.”

4

The Fear of Prospecting

“I don’t want to be that salesperson.”

Where it can come from

Maybe someone in your family burned relationships pushing something on everybody. Maybe you were taught it’s rude to talk about work, or you’re worried about what people will think of you.

What it does on the sales floor

You don’t tell people what you do. You keep your job off social media. You skip the networking event, and your friends buy from somebody else.

What it costs you

You miss the easiest sales there are: people who already know, like and trust you.

Your first step

Tell five people this week what you do and who you help. No pitch, just a conversation.

5

The Fear of Winning

“I’m just happy to be here.”

Where it can come from

Maybe you were compared to a brother or sister and learned to stay quiet. Maybe standing out got you teased. Or you lost so many times that not trying all the way felt safer than trying and losing.

What it does on the sales floor

You don’t go all in on the contest. You let other reps take the good ups. You cheer for everybody but yourself.

What it costs you

You never collect the wins that build real confidence, so every month feels like starting over.

Your first step

Pick one number to win this week, out loud, in front of the team. When you hit it, celebrate it.

6

The Fear of Asking for Referrals

“I don’t want to impose.”

Where it can come from

Maybe you learned that asking for help is a sign of weakness. Maybe pride runs in your family, or you asked someone for a favor once and they made you feel small.

What it does on the sales floor

You never ask. You hope happy customers will send people your way on their own. Most of them mean to, and most of them don’t.

What it costs you

You lose the warmest, cheapest and fastest business there is, from people who already love you.

Your first step

At the happiest moment, at delivery or when a claim gets paid, ask: “Who else do you know who’d want an experience like this?” Then stop talking.

7

The Fear of Closing

“If they say no, it means I’m not enough.”

Where it can come from

Maybe “no” meant more than no when you were growing up. Maybe you were called pushy for asking for what you wanted. Somewhere, a customer’s “no” started to feel like a judgment on you.

What it does on the sales floor

You present forever and wait for the customer to say yes. You give them an easy way out. You talk right past the moment you should have asked.

What it costs you

You do all the work, and somebody else closes the deal. Usually a competitor who simply asked.

Your first step

Ask one clear closing question in every presentation this week. Then be quiet and let them answer.
